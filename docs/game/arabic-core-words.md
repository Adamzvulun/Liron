# Arabic core words (E11)

- **Status:** drafted by Claude on the night of 2026-11-09, while Adam slept. He had said to keep going and make the calls he'd make. Every "my choice" below is his to change.
- **Epic:** [#150](https://github.com/Adamzvulun/Liron/issues/150), the second M3 sprint, after E12.

## The short version
- **The word path opens after the script course.** It starts once you pass "The Painted Sign" (chapter 1 of "The Long Road In"), when every letter is yours.
- **The first 300 words come from the deck's A1 words and verbs, in 10 chapters of 30, grouped by topic.** The most useful topics come first: greetings and people, then questions and family. Words 301–840 (the rest of A1) and 841–1,020 (the first A2 words) are in.
- **Each word is met with its native recording, its meaning, how it sounds in Hebrew letters, and a deck sentence when the deck has one.** The deck has few sentences, so this is the main open question (section 3).
- **Practice is the same four moves as the letters:** meet, listen, read, recall. FSRS schedules each word, and Glicko rates you in listen and read.
- **Each chapter brings a new regular to the innkeeper's table** ("The Long Road In" chapter 2 onward), with a trial at its end.
- **Grammar comes as short cards when a chapter needs it** (e.g. "no word for *is*" with the first sentences). They are built from the Madrasa beginners course's order and the deck's sentences, and every rule is checked against a free reference.

## 3. Sentences
The epic says "each shown in a sentence with audio". The deck can do that for about a third of the first 300 words (99 of them). The choices:

1. **Deck sentence when there is one, the word alone otherwise** (my choice for now). Everything you hear is a native speaker. No new source and nothing to buy.
2. **Our own sentences for the rest, as text only.** They would be written by Claude and checked against the deck and the free references (ADR 0012), with no audio. The browser's Arabic voices aren't Palestinian, so I'd rather leave them silent.
3. **Our own sentences with recorded audio:** this needs a speaker, and none is available.

I built option 1 first. On 2026-11-10 Adam chose to add option 2 as well (#164), and it is being built in batches of chapters:

- **Where:** `content/arabic/own-sentences.json` holds one sentence for each path word the deck has no sentence for. The sentences are ours, so the Arabic can live in `content/`, unlike the deck's own text (ADR 0011).
- **On the card:** the deck sentence still comes first, with its recording, and it is the only kind used for shadowing. A word with no deck sentence shows ours instead, with the Arabic, the Hebrew letters, the Latin and the English. It has no play button and carries the note "Our own sentence · no recording".
- **How they're written:** in the deck's Palestinian forms and its Latin scheme. Each sentence is short and uses words from the deck. Where possible it uses only words the learner has met by that chapter (path words plus deck sentences shown so far).
- **Checks:**
  - A scratch checker (kept out of the repo, because it reads the deck) flags any word that isn't in the deck, any word not yet met, and any sentence where the word itself is missing. Each flag is looked at by hand.
  - Tests check that every own sentence belongs to a path word with no deck sentence.
  - Tests check that every finished chapter is covered.
  - Adam spot-checks a sample.
- **Progress:**
  - Chapters I–XVIII are done (414 sentences).
  - Chapters XIX–XXXIV follow in later batches.

Micro-stories made only of words you know come in a later epic (vision, Arabic track).

## 4. A word lesson (#160)
- **Where:** a new station in the courtyard, the innkeeper's table (my choice): a low table under the left arcade with her open guest book, bread and a lamp. The slate moved a little toward the road to make room. It's locked until "The Painted Sign" is passed.
- **A session is 12 words:** due words first, then up to 5 new words from your chapter, then words you know (my choice, like the Codebook).
- **Meet**, for each new word: the Arabic with its marks, the native recording, our gloss, the sound in Hebrew letters, and the deck sentence if there is one.
- **Practice**, mixed:
  - **Listen:** hear the word, pick its meaning from 4.
  - **Read:** see the word, pick its meaning from 4. Vowel marks fade as in the letter lessons.
  - **Recall:** see the meaning, pick the Arabic from 4, then hear it.
- **Saving (ADR 0031):**
  - each word is an FSRS item `word:pcav:<n>`: right first time is Good, right after a miss is Hard, wrong is Again;
  - each answer is a result in the `listen` or `read` skill, so word practice moves the same ratings as the letters;
  - XP is about 10 a minute (economy);
  - events `word-session`, and `word-90` for 90% or better. Their daily contracts (2 word sessions, 15 gold; a session at 90%, 20 gold) are only offered once you've had a word session on an earlier day ([economy](economy.md#daily-contracts)).
- **The chapter gate:** like the letters, 90% in two sessions in a row, the second on a later day. Then the next chapter's words start coming.

## 6. Grammar cards (#162)
- **Just in time:** a chapter's card comes before its first session, once. It's saved with that session, so a card read and then abandoned comes back.
- **The grammar book:** every card you've read, behind the "Grammar book" button at the innkeeper's table.
- **What a card holds:** the rule in two to four lines of our own English, an endings table or a letter list where it helps, and 2–4 short deck sentences that show it, with their recordings. The cards are in `content/arabic/grammar.json`: our text and deck ids only (ADR 0011).
- **Order:** the Madrasa "ערבית מדוברת: מתחילים" structure is a reference only (ADR 0012).
- **The first four cards:**

| Ch. | Card | The rule | Deck sentences |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | No word for "is" | No *am/is/are* in the present; a question is the same words with a rising voice | I'm ready! · I'm Palestinian. · Are you married? |
| 2 | My, your, his: endings | -i, -ak, -ik, -u, -ha, -na, -kum, -hum; a word in -a shows a t (gahwa → gahwitak) | My name is __. · What's your name? · Where are you, Mom? · How would you like your coffee? |
| 3 | Counting things | 2: -ēn on the word; 3–10: short number + plural (with a t before a vowel: xamas tiyyām); 11 up: singular | Two minutes · three nights · five days · six classes |
| 4 | il- and the sun letters | il- before the word; before a sun letter the l becomes that sound; in Palestinian j often too | Good morning! · The sun is shining. · What time is it? · It's sunny. |

- **Checked against (2026-11-09):**
  - The deck itself, first: its romanization shows each rule (*ísmak*, *wíjhu*, *dagigtēn*, *xámas tiyyām*, *iššámis*, *ijjáww*).
  - [Levantine Arabic grammar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levantine_Arabic_grammar) (Wikipedia): no present copula, possessive suffixes, counting.
  - [Levantine Arabic/Numbers](https://en.wikibooks.org/wiki/Levantine_Arabic/Numbers) (Wikibooks): plural after 3–10, singular after 10.
  - [Palestinian-Jordanian Levantine Arabic: the definite article](https://www.adrosverse.com/?p=14466) and [Sun and moon letters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sun_and_moon_letters): j as a sun letter in Palestinian.
  - [Personal pronouns](https://www.adrosverse.com/?p=14474) (Adrosverse): -kum and -hum in Palestine and Jordan (-kon/-hon is Syrian and Lebanese).
- **Flagged for Adam:** j as a sun letter varies by speaker (usually yes where ج sounds like the *j* in French *jour*). The card says "often", and the deck's speaker does it.
- **Later chapters' cards** (verbs' present prefix b-, *bidd-* for "want", negation with *mā…š*, the past) come with chapters 5–10, the same way.
