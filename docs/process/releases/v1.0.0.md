# Waymark 1.0.0 — The Crossing

- **Status:** draft, written 2026-11-16 (PLAN task 1.10), rewritten 2026-11-17 when 1.0.0 grew to carry everything on `dev` ([ADR 0049](../../decisions/0049-one-zero-ships-what-dev-holds.md)). It becomes the text of the release PR (task 1.13). The date goes in when Adam merges.
- **What this release is:** the first one with something to learn, and everything built since. The live site has held only the empty city (v0.1.0) until now; 1.0.0 carries the whole game: both roads to their endings, the ledger, a city with sound and seasons, and friends.

## What a player gets

### The square
- A painted city square with five places to walk into, and your own character standing in it. The Arena lies beyond it.
- **One line that always says what to do next,** in the square, in both halls and at the end of every lesson. A first page greets a new player and points to the first step.
- Your level, XP, gold, gems, your streak and today's goal across the top. Streak shields save a missed day.
- **Small sounds and motion** that answer what you do, made in the browser. Each has its own switch in Settings, and nothing plays before your first tap.
- **Four weeks of the year are dressed for an occasion:** World Radio Day, World Amateur Radio Day, the olive harvest and World Arabic Language Day. The square flies their colors, and that week's contracts end in their own chest.

### The Signal Tower: Morse code
- **The Silent Beacon,** a story in ten chapters and 75 steps, from your first two sounds to a last message the far voice sends only once.
- Lessons for 41 characters by ear, at real speed from the first day, with the gaps closing as you improve.
- Instant Recognition: name the letter before the lamp goes out.
- Sending with your own hand, on the space bar or a tap pad, read back as you key.
- The Codebook: whole words, call signs and the operators' shorthand, by ear. The callsign rush.
- Five chapter trials, and three for rank: the Lamp Signal, the Night Watch and the Far Station.
- The Radio Room: contacts with six operators through noise and fading. With the site's AI switched on, they tell you their news and answer yours.
- **The finale, Forty Winters:** hear a message once at 20 words a minute, answer three questions about it, and key the reply. Passing makes you Keeper of the Hour.

### The Caravanserai: spoken Palestinian Arabic
- **The Long Road In,** a story in 115 chapters and 275 steps, from the letters to an evening's talk.
- The script from nothing: every letter and vowel mark, heard, read and written on the slate, with Hebrew as the bridge.
- The echo well for the sounds Hebrew doesn't have, and shadowing a speaker's voice.
- Nine sets of survival phrases at the coffee corner, with real recordings. "Say it" listens and tells you whether you'd be understood.
- 3,390 words in 113 chapters at the innkeeper's table, in sentences with audio, reviewed at the moment you're about to forget them. Each chapter has a regular who tells you about their life, a trial, and a grammar card.
- Sit and talk: a written chat in Arabic with any regular you've met, who remembers what you told them. Afterward the Coach shows how a Palestinian would have said your lines.
- **The finale, The Stranger at the Gate:** read a page of the innkeeper's guest book, write a stranger's name from memory on the slate, and tell the whole table the story of your road in Arabic. Passing makes you Keeper of the Table.

### After the end
- **The long evenings.** A finished road still has something every day: master contracts, a station of the day, and your personal bests to beat.

### Your House, the Market and the Notice Board
- Your House: build your character's look, dress it in what you've earned, and keep a pet at its side.
- **The ledger in Your House:** what you have really learned, in numbers. Letters and words known, reviews due, hearing and sending speed, days practiced, where you are on each road and how far each finale is.
- The Market: four wares a day for gold. Gold comes only from learning.
- The Notice Board: today's contracts, your quests, and the chests you've won.
- Ranks in both guilds, each with its own gear, and achievements with titles to wear.

### Friends (when you invite one)
None of this shows until you have a friend. Friends are invited by hand ([FRIENDS.md](../FRIENDS.md)).
- **The Fellowship:** swap friend codes in Your House, see each other's level, streak and ranks, and visit each other's house.
- **Word in the square:** friends' milestones are pinned on the Notice Board, and friends stand in the square. You can stop sharing yours.
- **The Arena:** challenge a friend to a callsign rush or a word round. Both scores and both bests are shown, and nobody is called the loser.
- **Together:** a weekly goal you work toward as a Fellowship, and gifts from the Market, paid in your own gold.
- **The wire:** key Morse to a friend live, each hearing the other's hand as it happens. Nothing is kept.

### The look
- Everything is painted in the browser: the square, both halls, and a scene behind every station.
- No screen stands on a plain blue background any more. The page is wood, stone, tile or cobbles, and sign-in stands before the city gate at dusk.

## Good to know
- **The live site starts you fresh,** at level 1. What you did on the dev site stays there (your choice, 2026-11-16).
- **The AI parts need the site's keys.** Settings → Services says "ready" or "not set up on this site" for Sit and talk, Say it and the radio operators. Without keys the game still works; those three stay quiet or scripted, and the Arabic finale's story waits for them.
- **The AI is shared fairly.** Each player has a daily allowance, and the site as a whole stays under what the free keys allow; a busy day ends with a friendly line, never a bill.
- **Sit and talk is in writing.** The regulars don't speak aloud yet (#342).
- **The Arabic words need the deck.** They come from the Lingualism deck, which only its buyer may use; they are not in the code. What a friend without it may learn is still to be decided (task 2.1).
